# NCC Under Attack — When Reform Meets Resistance

In recent weeks, a flurry of sensational headlines has sought to cast a dark cloud over the Nigerian Communications Commission (NCC), alleging corruption, favouritism, and administrative incompetence. The latest of these, published by Sahara Reporters on July 12, 2025, and another online publication Nairaland attempts to stitch together anonymous grievances, personal resignations, and discredited claims into a picture of institutional collapse.

Let us be clear: what is unfolding is not a scandal — it is the natural resistance to reform. And as history shows us, the loudest complaints often come from those who fear the loss of unearned privileges.

## Promotion Exercise: Facts Over Fiction

The so-called “promotion scandal” is neither scandalous nor unprecedented. Like every public institution guided by the Public Service Rules, the NCC conducted a structured, multi-tier promotion process in March 2025. The process was supervised by credible internal and external parties — including representatives from all six geopolitical zones and the Federal Character Commission.

Contrary to the narrative of bias or nepotism, the truth is simple: not everyone passed. And not every cadre had available vacancies. Promotions are based on performance, merit, and structured manpower planning — not emotional appeals or entitlement.

It is disheartening that some of those now crying foul either failed the exam or did not participate at all. Their disappointment, though understandable, cannot become the basis for public misinformation or reputational sabotage.


## Allegations Without Evidence

The article goes on to allege corruption and misuse of funds but fails to name a single transaction, contract, or figure. Where did the corruption occur? Which procurement process was flawed? What funds were mismanaged? In journalism and public accountability, evidence matters. Allegations without proof are nothing more than gossip dressed as outrage.

What is even more troubling is the contradictory logic used to justify these claims. The article accuses the CEO of “pocketing the board” while also demanding that the entire board and management be dissolved. If the board has indeed been silenced, why does the author seek its reconstitution with the same intensity as its removal?


## A Leadership of Proven Competence

The attempt to portray NCC’s management as incompetent does not align with the profile or track record of its leaders:

- Dr. Aminu Maida, Executive Vice Chairman/CEO, is a highly accomplished technocrat with a Ph.D. and a record of excellence in both public and private sectors. His appointment was based on merit and performance — and his results at the NCC speak for themselves.
- Engr. Abraham Oshademi, Executive Commissioner Technical Services, is a first-class graduate who rose through the ranks at the NCC and possesses unparalleled institutional knowledge and technical depth.
- Barr. Rimini Haraya Makama, Executive Commissioner Stakeholder Management, is a vibrant, visionary administrator and her career spans multiple sectors, including law enforcement and Technology. In 2014 she was named in the Forbes 20 youngest power women under 48 in Africa list.

These are not political appointees chasing contracts. They are reformers rebuilding a legacy institution that had, until recently, been weighed down by inefficiencies, waste, and outdated practices.


## Visible Impact, Real Results

The current NCC leadership has ushered in a new era of digital transformation and cost efficiency:

- Digitization of internal operations has slashed recurrent costs and improved service delivery.
- Long-abandoned Digital Industrial Parks in Kano and Ogbomosho have been completed and handed over as tech incubation hubs.
- ICT Parks at four leading universities — Ibadan, Owerri, Sokoto, and Daura — are now operational, enabling research, startups, and innovation.
- Pre-registered SIM cards have been wiped out from circulation, enhancing national security.
- The long-standing USSD dispute between banks and telcos was resolved under this administration’s watch.
- Fiber infrastructure vandalism has reduced due to more responsive regulatory oversight.
- Tariff adjustments have been balanced to protect consumers while ensuring the sustainability of telecom operators.

In recognition of these milestones, the NCC recently received international honour for its inclusive digital literacy programs. These are verifiable outcomes, not empty slogans.


## The Resignation Distraction

The article also tries to sensationalize the resignation of a staff member — the son of a former minister — as a “protest” against crisis at the NCC. Every Nigerian has the right to resign from a job. But elevating one individual’s career decision into a referendum on an entire organization’s future is disingenuous at best, and mischievous at worst.


## A Familiar Pattern: When Change Threatens Comfort

What we are witnessing is a familiar pattern. An entrenched culture of entitlement is being dismantled. The “old ways” — trips without merit, promotions without exams, and influence without accountability — are no longer tolerated. And so the backlash has come, cloaked in the language of victimhood, but rooted in discomfort with change.

The NCC is not collapsing. It is transforming. And transformation often hurts those unwilling to adapt.


## Conclusion

The Nigerian Communications Commission remains one of the strongest pillars of our digital economy. Under the renewed vision of President Bola Ahmed Tinubu and the competent leadership of Dr. Aminu Maida and his team, the commission is delivering impact where it matters — from rural connectivity to cybersecurity, from digital inclusion to stakeholder confidence.


Instead of tearing down what is working, we should be asking: How do we sustain and scale this progress? Because in a digital world, the collapse of a regulator like NCC is not just a loss for staff — it’s a risk to our national future.
